I am a computational engineer currently working at Novelis in Atlanta, building 
simulation pipelines for aluminum manufacturing processes. Before this I completed 
an MS in Computational Science and Engineering at Georgia Tech, where my work 
focused on multiphysics modeling of vacuum induction melting.

My technical interests sit at the intersection of high-fidelity simulation, 
scientific machine learning, and data assimilation. The long-term goal is building 
digital twins for manufacturing processes that combine physics-based models with 
learned surrogates and real-time data.

This blog is where I work through ideas in computational physics, numerics, and ML. 
Occasionally also philosophy, since the questions about what it means to model 
something well do not stay purely technical for long.
